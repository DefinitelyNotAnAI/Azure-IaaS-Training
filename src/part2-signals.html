Part 2 — Confirm Signals

Verify your VM's telemetry and support tickets are landing in the shared Fabric data layer.

What you're about to do

Your legacy support app has been running on your VM since Part 1. It has been emitting two streams of data to the shared data layer:

  • Telemetry (Plane A) — per-operation metrics: latency, errors, throughput — landing in the shared Eventhouse (workshop-db, Telemetry table), filtered to your slot by a per-slot KQL function.
  • Support tickets (Plane B) — simulated customer complaints — landing in the shared Lakehouse (workshop-lakehouse, Tickets table).

In this challenge you will confirm both streams are arriving and get comfortable with the Fabric query surface you'll use for the rest of Part 2.

Must-do — Confirm telemetry in Eventhouse

  1. Open the Fabric workspace using the button above.
  2. In the workspace, click on workshop-eventhouse, then open the workshop-db KQL database.
  3. Click Explore your data (or open the KQL queryset). Copy and run the starter query below — it uses your per-slot function which filters to only your data.
  4. Confirm you see at least a few rows with your SlotId, timestamps, service names, and latency values.
// Confirm your telemetry signals (replace <> if needed)
SlotTelemetry_<>()
| take 10
| project Timestamp, Service, Operation, Region, LatencyMs, ErrorCount, IsAnomaly
| order by Timestamp desc
No rows? Check that the VM extension installed successfully (Part 1 validation page) and that at least 5 minutes have passed since the app started. If the issue persists, raise your hand — the facilitator can check the Eventstream ingestion logs.

Must-do — Confirm tickets in Lakehouse

  1. In the Fabric workspace, open workshop-lakehouse.
  2. Under Tables, find the Tickets table.
  3. Click Preview (or open a Notebook/SQL endpoint) and filter by SlotId = '<>'.
  4. Confirm you see support tickets with your slot ID, customer names, and description text. Note that the descriptions are intentionally messy — they're simulated user complaints.

You can also query Tickets from the KQL surface using the per-slot function:

SlotTickets_<>()
| take 10
| project Timestamp, TicketId, CustomerTenant, Description, IncidentId
| order by Timestamp desc

Explore: understand what the chaos engine injected

Run this query to get a summary of your telemetry grouped by anomaly flag. Normal operation and injected incidents will show as separate bands.

// Anomaly vs normal traffic summary
SlotTelemetry_<>()
| summarize Count = count(), AvgLatencyMs = avg(LatencyMs)
    by bin(Timestamp, 10m), IsAnomaly
| order by Timestamp asc

You should see normal low-latency periods punctuated by anomaly spikes. Keep a mental note of the spike timestamps — you'll find their ticket counterparts in Challenge 2.
